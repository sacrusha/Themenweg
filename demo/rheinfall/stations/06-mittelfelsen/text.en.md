Two rocks stand in the middle of the falls; the Swiss flag flies on the larger one. They are remains of the steep limestone flank of the old river channel where the falls formed. Over thousands of years the water has washed around them and hollowed them out, but not worn them away.

This occupied Goethe more than anything else. "The most wonderful phenomenon of the Rhine Falls to me is therefore the rocks that have survived so long in its middle," he wrote in 1797, since they were surely of the same stone as the banks.

From this landing, boats go out into the basin, which is up to 13 metres deep at the foot of the falls, and to the large rock, which visitors can climb. Above the falls, by contrast, boating is banned between the Flurlingen bridge and the falls. A few kayakers have still dared the drop.

The Photochrom print of around 1900 shows a rowing boat in front of the rocks. The view has stayed the same; only the boats have grown larger.
