The water wheel at the old mill recalls the centuries in which the falls drove mills. Goethe noted "the mills over there" from the other bank in 1797. Right beside it the Neuhausen power station is at work. It produces about five megawatts and uses only a small part of the water: little, compared with the power plunging down here.

That it stayed this way is the result of a long dispute. In 1887 the Neher family's firm applied to take 75 cubic metres of water a second from the falls on the right bank. The Swiss Alpine Club and many other associations protested, and the governments of Zurich and Schaffhausen had two experts measure the flows anew. It was an early case of a Swiss landscape being defended against its use.

More plans followed: in 1913 an international competition for a shipping route from Basel to Lake Constance, in 1961 a ship tunnel about 550 metres long to bypass the falls. None was built. The question is still not settled: in 2024 the Zurich government proposed providing in the cantonal structure plan for a power station that would divert up to 125 cubic metres of water a second in front of Schloss Laufen and lead it past the falls underground.
