Between Neuhausen am Rheinfall and Laufen-Uhwiesen the Rhine plunges 23 metres over a rock ledge 150 metres wide. To boatmen the falls were an obstacle, to millers and manufacturers a source of power, to painters and poets a picture. Each of these roles was fought over, and some questions are still open.

The walk leads around the falls in ten stations: down the right bank to the Schlössli Wörth, downstream to the Nohl bridge, up the left bank to Schloss Laufen and back across the railway bridge to the start. It is about three and a half kilometres long, with steps and short climbs on both banks.

Each station comes with old views: engravings and paintings, coloured Photochrom prints from around 1900, early colour photographs from 1911 and 1923, aerial photographs by the aviation pioneer Walter Mittelholzer, and pictures of today. Under "Map of 1880" the map shows the area as the national survey recorded it then.

The way down from Schloss Laufen to the platforms at the water charges admission. The boats to the rock in the middle do not run all year.
