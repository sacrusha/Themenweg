Laufen is first mentioned in 858. The castle was the ancestral seat of the barons of Laufen and afterwards had many owners, among them the Bishop of Constance, the monastery of Allerheiligen in Schaffhausen and the counts of Kyburg. In 1544 the city of Zurich bought the castle and the bailiwick and extended the castle with a ring wall and a gate tower. Its rule ended in 1798, when French troops marched in.

Then the painters moved in. Johann Heinrich Bleuler the Elder set up a workshop here from 1799 to 1804. His son Louis Bleuler leased the castle in 1832, bought it in 1845 and ran a painting school, whose views of Switzerland travellers took home as souvenirs. The falls outside the window were their best motif.

Since 1941 the castle has belonged to the canton of Zurich. Today it houses an exhibition and a restaurant, and until 2019 also a youth hostel. The way down to the platforms at the water charges admission.
