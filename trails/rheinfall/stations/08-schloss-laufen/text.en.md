Schloss Laufen is first mentioned in 858. At that time the castle was the seat of the barons of Laufen; its lord held the Vogtei, the jurisdiction, over the surrounding villages, while the land belonged to the Bishop of Constance. In 1544 the city of Zurich bought the castle and rebuilt it in the following years. Its rule ended in 1798, when French troops marched in.

Then the painters moved in. Johann Heinrich Bleuler the Elder set up a workshop here from 1799 to 1804. His son Louis Bleuler leased the castle in 1830, bought it in 1845 and ran a painting school, whose views of Switzerland travellers took home as souvenirs. The falls outside the window were their best motif.

Since 1941 the castle has belonged to the canton of Zurich. Today it houses a museum and a restaurant, and until 2019 also a youth hostel. The way down to the platforms at the water charges admission.

David Herrliberger showed the castle above the falls around 1750, a century after Merian's engraving. Both make the rock higher than it is.
