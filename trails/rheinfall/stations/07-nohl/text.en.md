Below the falls the Rhine grows quiet. Goethe found it "calm, shallow and insignificant" here. It is no longer shallow: the Rheinau power station, a few kilometres downstream, dams the river back as far as the basin below the falls.

This power station was fought over throughout Switzerland. The Federal Council had granted the concession in 1944, and construction began in 1952. Opponents collected signatures; among the signatories of an appeal were Hermann Hesse and Carl Jacob Burckhardt. The popular initiative to protect the river landscape was rejected in 1954 by 69 percent, and the power station was built.

For fish the falls have always been the end of the journey. None gets up, except the eel: it crawls past the drop over land, along damp places. Salmon once swam up from the North Sea as far as here and were caught below the falls. Today power stations block their way long before they reach the Rhine Falls.
