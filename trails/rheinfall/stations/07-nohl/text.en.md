Below the falls the Rhine grows quiet. The Rheinau power station, a few kilometres downstream, dams the river back as far as the basin below the falls; the Rhine Falls lost about two metres of height as a result.

This power station was fought over throughout Switzerland. The Federal Council had granted the concession in 1944, and construction began in 1952. Opponents submitted a petition with 160,000 signatures, and thousands demonstrated in Rheinau. The popular initiative to protect the river landscape was rejected in 1954 by 69 percent; only the canton of Schaffhausen accepted it. The power station was built.

For fish the falls have always been the end of the journey. Upstream, at most a few eels get past them. Salmon once swam up from the sea as far as here and were caught below the falls. In the 20th century they died out in the Rhine, above all because of the power stations. Reintroduced salmon still get past the power stations on the Upper Rhine only now and then.
