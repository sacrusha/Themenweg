Up here the Rhine still flows in broad and calm, on average about 373 cubic metres of water a second, many times that in a flood. A few metres further on it drops 23 metres.

The Rhine Falls are young. They formed 14,000 to 17,000 years ago, at the end of the last ice age, when the Rhine took a new course here. It flowed over hard limestone, the Malm of the Jura mountains, and then met an old channel from an earlier ice age, filled with loose gravel. The water cleared the gravel easily and the limestone hardly at all. The step formed at this boundary.

Why have the falls retreated so little since? The river carries hardly any rubble, because Lake Constance holds back what the Rhine brings from the Alps. Without sand and stones to grind with, clear water wears away rock only slowly.

The colour photograph of 1923 shows the river above the falls. It is one of the early colour photographs that the Paris banker Albert Kahn had taken in many countries for his "Archives of the Planet".
