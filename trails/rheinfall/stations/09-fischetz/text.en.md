Down here, on the platform above the water, you are closest to the falls. Already in 1797 visitors climbed down from the castle onto a wooden platform above the water, and from there Goethe watched the falls.

In December 1779 Goethe drew the falls. In September 1797 he spent a whole day writing down what he saw: green currents, spray shimmering purple, a rainbow that stood "with its calm foot in the immense spray and foam". A line from "The Diver", a poem his friend Schiller had only just written, came to his mind: "It seethes and it boils and it roars and it hisses". In the evening, he noted, "the onlooker feared to be overcome by the excess, and as a human being expected a catastrophe at any moment". Decades later, at the beginning of the second part of "Faust", he has Faust stand before a waterfall with a rainbow arching over it.

In 1846 the poet Eduard Mörike began his poem "Am Rheinfall" (At the Rhine Falls) with the lines: "Hold your heart fast, o wanderer, in mighty hands! Mine, trembling with delight, almost plunged from me."
