The railway bridge was built in 1855 and 1856 under the chief engineer Carl Ruland and opened in 1857, for the Rheinfallbahn from Winterthur to Schaffhausen. It is 177 metres long and rests on nine large stone arches spanning 12 to 18 metres, plus a small one on the left bank. It crosses the river at an angle, about ten metres above the water, shortly before the river drops.

People have only walked here since 1959. From December 1957 to March 1959 the arches were strengthened and footpaths 1.4 metres wide were added on both sides. Since then the way has led across the river, with a view down onto the edge of the falls.

Today S-Bahn trains stop at both ends, at Neuhausen Rheinfall and at Schloss Laufen am Rheinfall. The railway that once opened up the falls to travellers has itself become part of the view: in many pictures since 1857 a train crosses the arches.
