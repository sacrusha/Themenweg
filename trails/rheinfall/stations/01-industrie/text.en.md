Where the S-Bahn stops today, Neuhausen's industry stood close to the water. As early as the 17th century a blast furnace on the right bank smelted bog iron ore, and in the early 19th century the ironworks came to life once more. Its owner, Johann Georg Neher, secured the rights to the water of the falls. That laid the ground for everything that followed.

In 1853 Friedrich Peyer im Hof, Johann Conrad Neher and Heinrich Moser founded the Swiss Wagon Factory here, which grew into SIG. From the start, its machines were driven by the Rhine.

Then came a new metal. In 1886 the Frenchman Paul Héroult had patented a process for extracting aluminium from a melt with electric current. It takes electricity in large quantities, which the Rhine Falls provided, along with empty buildings in the old ironworks. The company was founded in 1887 and production began in 1888: the first aluminium works in Europe, later the Aluminium-Industrie-Aktien-Gesellschaft (AIAG) and Alusuisse. In 1944 the Neuhausen works were converted into a research centre.

Johann Heinrich Bleuler painted the factories on the right bank in 1837, right next to the falls. Even then the Rhine Falls were not only a natural spectacle but also a workplace.
