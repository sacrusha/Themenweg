Where the S-Bahn stops today, Neuhausen's industry stood close to the water. From 1731 to 1771 a smelting works on the right bank processed bog iron ore from the Südranden hills. In 1810 Johann Georg Neher took up smelting again, and with the ironworks he and his heirs held the water power of the falls. That laid the ground for everything that followed.

In 1853 Friedrich Peyer im Hof, Johann Conrad Neher and Heinrich Moser founded the Swiss Wagon Factory here, which grew into SIG.

Then came a new metal. In 1886 the Frenchman Paul Héroult had patented a process for extracting aluminium from a melt with electric current. It takes electricity in large quantities, which the Rhine Falls provided, along with the buildings of the old ironworks. In 1887 the Swiss Metallurgical Company, founded for the purpose, leased the water power and the buildings; production began in 1888, and that same year the company became the Aluminium-Industrie-Aktien-Gesellschaft (AIAG), later Alusuisse. Neuhausen was thus among the first works to extract aluminium with electricity. In 1944 the firm moved production from the falls to the Valais; its research stayed in Neuhausen.

Johann Heinrich Bleuler the Younger painted the factories on the right bank in 1837, right next to the falls. Even then the Rhine Falls were not only a natural spectacle but also a workplace.
