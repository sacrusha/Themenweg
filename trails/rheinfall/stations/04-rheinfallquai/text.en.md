From the quay you see the falls head on, with Schloss Laufen on its rock above. It is the view painters and photographers have chosen for centuries.

The earliest known depiction is hidden in a picture that is not about the Rhine Falls at all. Around 1515 to 1520 Joachim Patinir painted a Baptism of Christ and set a waterfall in the landscape behind, in which the Rhine Falls can be recognised. In 1642 Matthäus Merian engraved the falls in copper for his topography of Switzerland.

In the 18th century travellers sought the sublime in nature, and the Rhine Falls became a must. Philip James de Loutherbourg painted spray and rock as drama in 1788. William Turner came several times; his painting of about 1805 is considered the most important depiction of the falls. The young John Ruskin drew the water closely, strand by strand.

The pictures show less what the falls looked like than how people wanted to see them: higher, wilder, darker. The comparison is worth making. The falls in front of you have stayed the same; the banks have not.
