From the quay you see the falls head on, with Schloss Laufen on its rock above. It is the view painters and photographers have chosen for centuries.

The earliest known depiction is a woodcut in Sebastian Münster's "Cosmographia" of 1544. In 1642 Matthäus Merian engraved the falls in copper for his topography of Switzerland.

In the 18th century travellers sought the sublime in nature, and the Rhine Falls became a must. Philip James de Loutherbourg painted spray and rock as drama in 1788. William Turner drew the falls in 1802, exhibited an oil painting of them in London in 1806 and painted them again in watercolours around 1841. John Ruskin drew them in 1842 and later used them as an example of how hard moving water is to paint.

Compare the pictures with the falls in front of you: each also shows how its painter wanted to see them. The falls have hardly changed; the banks all the more.
